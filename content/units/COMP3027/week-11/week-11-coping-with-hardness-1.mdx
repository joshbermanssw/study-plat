## Summary

Lecture 11 surveys what to do when a problem is NP-hard: give up on one of (a) optimality, (b) polynomial time, or (c) full generality. Focus is on restricted-input poly-time algorithms (DP on trees) and a glimpse at exact exponential algorithms.

- **Three-way trade-off:** approximation algorithms / heuristics; exact exponential algorithms / ILP; restricted classes (trees, bipartite, planar, bounded treewidth) and parametrised algorithms.
- **Independent Set on trees:** greedy by leaves works. Key exchange-argument observation: there is a max IS containing every leaf v (else swap parent u out for v).
- **Weighted Independent Set on trees:** greedy fails; DP on tree. Subproblems OPT_in(v), OPT_out(v) = best IS in subtree rooted at v with v included / excluded; recurrences OPT_out(v) = Σ max(OPT_in(c), OPT_out(c)) over children c, OPT_in(v) = w_v + Σ OPT_out(c). O(n) post-order traversal.
- **Restricted-instance pattern:** structure (a tree, a DAG, bounded width) lets you DP over substructures even when the general problem is NP-hard.
- **Other coping tools (preview):** parametrised complexity FPT in parameter k, branch-and-bound, local search.

**Exam focus:** design a DP on a tree (define in/out subproblems indexed by node, give recurrences, base case at leaves, O(n) bound) and justify why the same approach fails on general graphs.
