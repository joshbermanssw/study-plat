## Summary

Lecture 2 introduces greedy algorithms — make a locally optimal choice at each step — and the two canonical proof techniques (exchange argument, structural lower bound) that justify them. First major algorithm-design paradigm in the W2–W10 reduction roadmap.

* **Fractional Knapsack:** sort by benefit/weight ratio, take greedily; O(n log n). Correctness by exchange argument (swap a fractional unit between items with different b/w ratios → strictly improves OPT, contradiction).

* **Interval Scheduling:** Earliest-Finish-Time is optimal in O(n log n); ESF, shortest-interval, fewest-conflicts all give counter-examples. Proof via "stays ahead" / exchange + induction.

* **Scheduling to minimise lateness:** Earliest-Deadline-First is optimal. Exchange argument: any inversion (i,k) with d\_i &lt; d\_k scheduled k-before-i can be swapped without increasing max lateness; iterate.

* **Interval Partitioning:** sort by start time, assign to earliest-free room; number of rooms = depth (structural lower bound).

* **General template:** define greedy → try simple counter-examples → exchange or structural-bound proof.

**Exam focus:** prove correctness via a clean exchange argument (define inversion, swap, show cost does not increase) or derive the structural bound matching the greedy output.
