## Summary

Lecture 10 formalises the complexity classes P, NP, and NP-complete; Cook-Levin gives the first NP-complete problem (CIRCUIT-SAT), and a chain of reductions populates the rest of the NP-complete zoo.

- **P:** decision problems with a deterministic poly-time algorithm (e.g., MST, PRIMES, RNA secondary structure as a decision problem).
- **NP (formal):** decision problem X admits a poly-time certifier C such that I is a yes-instance ⇔ ∃ poly-length certificate t with C(I, t) = YES. No short proof exists for NO-instances in general — asymmetry is essential.
- **NP-complete:** Y ∈ NP and X ≤_p Y for every X ∈ NP. Implies Y ∈ P ⇔ P = NP.
- **Cook-Levin theorem:** CIRCUIT-SAT is NP-complete; every poly-time certifier becomes a poly-size Boolean circuit. SAT, 3-SAT inherit hardness; then the chain 3-SAT ≤_p IS ≤_p VC ≤_p Set-Cover, 3-SAT ≤_p Hamiltonian-Cycle ≤_p TSP, 3-SAT ≤_p Subset-Sum, etc.
- **Strategy to prove NP-completeness of Y:** (1) show Y ∈ NP via a certifier; (2) pick a known NP-complete X and give a Karp reduction X ≤_p Y.

**Exam focus:** state the certifier (input, certificate, runtime) for NP-membership, then construct and verify a Karp reduction from a known NP-complete problem. Memorise the canonical 3-SAT clause-gadget reductions.
