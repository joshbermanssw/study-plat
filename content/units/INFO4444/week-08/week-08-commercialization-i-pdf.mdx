## Summary

Week 8 lecture launching Module 3 on Commercialisation: what startups are, how they differ from incumbents, and how to discover a workable business model.

- **Startup definitions**: Steve Blank — "a temporary organisation in search of a scalable, repeatable, profitable business model"; Eric Ries — "a human institution delivering a new product/service under extreme uncertainty."
- **Established vs startup**: incumbents *execute* a known business model with KPIs and forecasts; startups *search* under uncertainty with hypotheses and pivots.
- **Sources of startup ideas (Paul Graham)**: solve a problem you have yourself, build something a small number of people want a lot, live at the leading edge of a field, *notice* rather than think up.
- **Customer Development (Blank)**: discovery → validation → creation → company building, contrasted with the linear New Product Introduction model.
- **Lean Startup (Ries)**: build–measure–learn loop with MVPs; pivot or persevere based on validated learning.

**Exam focus**: short answer on the four phases of Customer Development and the Blank vs Ries definitions, plus an essay contrasting startup search with incumbent execution and explaining why incumbents struggle to act like startups.
