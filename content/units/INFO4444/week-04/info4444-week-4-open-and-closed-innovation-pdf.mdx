## Summary

Week 4 lecture opening Module 2: how firms shifted from closed in-house R&D to **open innovation** (Chesbrough), and what that means for IP and time-to-market.

- **Closed innovation**: traditional one-way innovation funnel; all R&D in-house; reliant on spillovers.
- **Drivers of openness**: worker mobility, outsourcing, globalisation, ICT, VC funding for startups → **Joy's Law** ("most bright people don't work for you").
- **Open Innovation (Chesbrough)**: "purposive inflows and outflows of knowledge to accelerate internal innovation and expand external markets." Revised 2014 definition emphasises managed cross-boundary flows tied to the business model.
- **Three modes**: outside-in (integrate external knowledge, e.g. embedding OpenAI Whisper), inside-out (licence/spin out IP, e.g. ARM licensing chip designs), and coupled (alliances, co-creation).
- **Australian context**: 2024 Global Innovation Index rank 23; A$14.4B government R&D support (0.52% GDP); R&D shifting away from large incumbents toward SMEs.

**Exam focus**: short answer defining open vs closed innovation and the three modes, plus an essay asking you to classify a real company's strategy (e.g. P&G Connect+Develop, ARM, OpenAI partnerships).
