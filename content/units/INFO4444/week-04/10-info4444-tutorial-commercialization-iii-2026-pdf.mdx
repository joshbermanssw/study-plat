## Summary

Week 10 tutorial (in the week-4 folder) on the Business Model Canvas (Osterwalder, 2008), the central commercialisation tool of Module 3, applied to the **GrowSmart** smart urban gardening case study.

- **Nine BMC components**: customer segments, value propositions, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partnerships, cost structure.
- **Built empirically**: 470 practitioners in 45 countries; later variants include the Lean Canvas.
- **Value Proposition Canvas pairs with BMC**: map customer pains/gains to pain relievers and gain creators; reflect on the value proposition pyramid.
- **GrowSmart case**: identify segments (urban renters, hobbyists, schools), revenue model (subscription, marketplace fees, hardware), partnerships (IoT vendors, plant suppliers), and channels (app store, community).
- **Tools**: Strategyzer template, Canvanizer, course PPT — used directly in the assignment.

**Exam focus**: applied question — produce a BMC for a given hypothetical startup, naming all nine blocks and justifying the value proposition. Markers reward coherence across blocks (e.g. channels matching segments), not just listing.

1 INFO4444 Computing 4 Innovation Week 10 Tutorial 10: Business Model Canvas Business Model Canvas is a global standard used by millions of people in companies of all sizes. You can use the canvas to describe, design, challenge and pivot your business model. It works in conjunction with the Value Proposition Canvas and other strategic management and execution tools and processes. It was initially proposed by Alexander Osterwalder in 2008 with several iterations, such as the Lean Canvas, appearing later. BMC was developed by studying hundreds of business models (involved 470 practitioners in 45 countries) and extracted key aspects into a model to make a common framework and tested it. There are 9 components of the BMC as shown in the template below. 2 Case study: Bringing a Smart Urban Gardening App to Market: You are part of a startup team building GrowSmart, a smart urban gardening app that helps city dwellers grow vegetables using IoT sensors, personalized AI care plans, and a community marketplace. As a group, create a BMC for GrowSmart and discuss the following questions: 1. Who are the primary customer segments for GrowSmart? 2. What are the most important pains and gains for these segments, and how does GrowSmart address them (Pain Relievers and Gain Creators)? 3. What is the value proposition for GrowSmart, and how can it evolve over time? Map how the product features deliver value and reflect on the value proposition pyramid. 4. What should GrowSmart’s revenue model and key partnerships look like? 5. What are the major types of channels and their functions? 6. Identify the major types and roles of the block ‘customer relationships? Work with your group on the BMC in your assignment.
