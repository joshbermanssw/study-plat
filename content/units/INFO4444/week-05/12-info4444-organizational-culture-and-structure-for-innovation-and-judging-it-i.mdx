## Summary

Week 12 reading (in the week-5 folder), closing Module 4: how organisational **culture** and **structure** either enable or strangle innovation, and how to judge IT innovation.

- **Taylorism / Scientific Management**: efficient for repetitive work but hostile to creative, uncertain innovation tasks.
- **Where good ideas come from (Steven Johnson)**: **liquid networks**, the **slow hunch**, and connecting-not-protecting drive idea generation; market and non-market, individual and networked innovations all matter historically.
- **McKinsey culture for a digital age**: appetite for risk, customer-centricity, data-driven decisions, busting silos, accountability, resource reallocation — Amazon as exemplar.
- **Structural trade-offs (Schilling)**: large firms — less R&D efficiency, more bureaucracy, but resources; small firms — agile but resource-constrained. Mechanistic vs organic structures; centralisation, formalisation, standardisation.
- **Judging IT innovation**: criteria include novelty, impact, feasibility, scalability, sustainability, alignment to business model.

**Exam focus**: essay contrasting Taylorist culture with innovation culture, plus an applied question evaluating an organisation's structure (e.g. why Google's 20% time, Amazon's two-pizza teams) against Schilling's dimensions.
