## Summary

Week 5 tutorial applying distributed-innovation concepts (crowdsourcing, FOSS, open data) to self-driving cars, comparing Waymo or Apollo against OpenPilot (comma.ai).

- **Crowdsourcing and product platforms**: Apollo (Baidu) operates an open autonomous-driving platform; OpenPilot leverages a community of contributors; Waymo is more closed but uses platform partners.
- **Motivations despite competition**: open innovation accelerates data collection, lowers safety-validation cost, attracts talent, and sets de facto standards in a regulation-heavy field.
- **Open data strategy**: combine government open data (maps, traffic, accident records) with self-released datasets (Waymo Open Dataset, Apollo's perception data) to fuel research and goodwill.
- **FOSS benefits**: faster iteration, external bug fixes, ecosystem lock-in, and recruitment; OpenPilot's release demonstrates how a small player gains scale by being open.
- **Tesla contrast**: keeps vertically integrated, closed-source stack and proprietary fleet data — strategy is data moat plus brand, not community.

**Exam focus**: short essay comparing open vs closed strategies in a real industry. Markers reward naming the distributed-innovation mode and tying motivations to industry conditions (regulation, data-hungry ML, capital intensity).
