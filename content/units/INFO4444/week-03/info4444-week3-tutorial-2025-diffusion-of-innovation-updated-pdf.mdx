## Summary

Week 3 tutorial applying Christensen's Disruptive Innovation theory to Coursera and the wider Massive Open Online Course (MOOC) phenomenon in education.

- **Problem solved**: Coursera makes high-quality university content accessible globally, cheaply, asynchronously — addressing cost, geographic, and time barriers of traditional tertiary education.
- **Enabling technologies**: video streaming, cloud hosting, LMS platforms, mobile delivery, peer-grading, automated assessment, analytics, and increasingly AI tutoring.
- **Type of disruption**: classify it — is it **new-market** (turning non-consumers, e.g. working adults in developing economies, into learners) or **low-end** (cheaper substitute pulling away university customers)? Strong answers argue mostly new-market with growing low-end pressure.
- **Identify the alternatives that existed pre-Coursera**: traditional degrees, evening classes, textbooks, corporate training.
- **Defend under challenge**: each group fields counterpoints, so map users → alternatives → disruption type explicitly.

**Exam focus**: short essay applying Christensen's framework to a specific IT-enabled service. Markers reward naming the disruption type and grounding it in who the customer was before the innovation existed.
