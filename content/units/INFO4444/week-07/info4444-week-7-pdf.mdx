## Summary

Week 7 lecture wrapping Module 2: **user innovation**, **platform ecosystems**, and **IP protection** as the final pillars of distributed innovation.

- **User innovation (von Hippel)**: users — not producers — are often the source of important innovations. ~80% of major scientific instrument innovations came from users; same pattern in sports and IT.
- **Lead users**: face general-market needs early, benefit significantly from a solution, sit at leading-edge trends. They are *not* the same as a firm's biggest customers. Identify them to escape "functional fixedness" of typical market research.
- **Critiques**: user-led innovation can produce sameness and miss disruptive moves (Apple cited as deliberately non-user-led).
- **Platform ecosystems**: platform owner + complementors + users; sharing-economy examples (Uber, Airbnb) illustrate two-sided markets and network effects.
- **IP protection**: patents (utility, design), copyright, trademarks, trade secrets; choose mechanism based on what's being protected and openness strategy.

**Exam focus**: short answer defining lead users vs typical users, plus an essay applying user innovation and IP protection to a platform business — markers reward naming concrete mechanisms (e.g. App Store review as curation, patents around platform APIs).
